\section{Evaluation Framework}
\label{sec:eval}

\subsection{Benchmark}
\label{sec:benchmark}

The benchmark contains 500 Traditional Chinese questions, 100 in each of
five categories. Rounds 0--2 (Sections~\ref{sec:round0}--\ref{sec:fusion})
used the original 100 questions, 20 per category, with ground truth
approved by annotators with biblical expertise; Round~3
(Section~\ref{sec:round3}) uses all 500. The five categories are:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{VERSE\_LOOKUP} --- exact reference lookup (``What does
Romans 3:23 say?'');
\item \textbf{TOPIC} --- thematic aggregation (``What does the Bible
teach about forgiveness?'');
\item \textbf{PERSON} --- character-centric questions, often
multi-person (``What was the relationship between Elijah and Elisha?'');
\item \textbf{EVENT} --- event-narrative questions (``What happened at
the Last Supper?'');
\item \textbf{GENERAL} --- open questions requiring knowledge
integration, including cross-book prophecy--fulfillment.
\end{itemize}

Each item carries \code{reference} (gold pericope-level provenance),
\code{expected\_answer\_points} (a checklist of content points), and
\code{reference\_answer} (a model answer). The taxonomy deliberately
spans exact matching, semantic search, entity-centric retrieval, and
cross-book integration, so that route-level behavior is observable per
category.

Once Round~2 had saturated the original 100 (Section~\ref{sec:fusion}),
400 questions were added on July 11, 2026 --- after the Round~2
repairs --- in 19 diagnostic families of size $n \ge 8$
(Table~\ref{tab:benchmark}); we call them the \emph{expansion} set and
the first 100 the \emph{original} (legacy) set. Each family aims at a
failure mode identified in Sections~\ref{sec:audit}--\ref{sec:fusion}.
The \emph{longtail\_*} families ask about books, chapters, persons, and
events that head questions never reach, \emph{head\_cold} about cold
passages of head books, and \emph{parable} about the parables.
\emph{Paraphrase} gives a quotation without its reference, which
bypasses R1; \emph{title\_coref} names a person only by title (the
missing-coreference gap) and \emph{disambiguation} asks about
homonymous persons; \emph{trajectory} and \emph{multi\_chapter} require
cross-chapter aggregation (the PERSON\_004 family); \emph{split\_pericope}
targets persons in chunk-split pericopes (mechanism M3); \emph{temporal}
asks for event ordering and \emph{surface\_form} uses modern labels
absent from the RCUV text. Five cross-book multi-anchor families populate
the GENERAL\_006/008 family of Section~\ref{sec:discussion}. All 400
references were machine-validated against the corpus, every question
was content-checked against the corpus text in an LLM-assisted review
(49 corrections), and new reference answers use the corpus's own
spellings. The original 100 are unchanged, so they remain a comparable
subset across all four rounds.

\subsection{Metrics}
\label{sec:evalmetrics}

Sixteen metrics (counting the two faithfulness judges separately) fall
into four families:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Deterministic retrieval metrics} (no LLM in scoring;
recomputable bit-exactly from archived outputs): hit rate, recall@5,
precision@5, F1@5, MRR, MAP@5, NDCG@5,
computed program\-matically against the gold references with each gold
pericope or chapter range counted as one unit; and, added in July 2026,
\emph{verse recall@5} (the fraction of gold verses retrieved, ranges
expanded verse by verse) and \emph{anchor coverage@5} (the fraction of
gold chapter-level anchors reached by at least one retrieved passage;
not to be confused with the knowledge graph's \emph{entity} anchor
coverage of Section~\ref{sec:p0}).
\item \textbf{RAGAS generation metrics}~\cite{es2023ragas}:
faithfulness, answer relevancy, context recall, and answer correctness,
judged by a locally served Gemma~4 26B. Since September 2026
faithfulness is reported by two judges that share one statement
decomposition: \emph{zh}, a Traditional Chinese NLI rubric that also
sees the user question, and \emph{strict}, RAGAS's default NLI prompt.
\item \textbf{Answer coverage} (ours): an LLM judge scores each expected
answer point as covered (1.0), partial (0.5), or missing (0.0); coverage
is the mean. Unlike F1-style correctness it is a \emph{pure recall}
metric --- it rewards completeness without penalizing extra detail,
complementing faithfulness as a hallucination check.
\item \textbf{Semantic similarity}: embedding cosine between generated
and reference answers.
\end{enumerate}

Five measurement pitfalls, encountered concretely in this project,
qualify the numbers we report. (i) Per-question judge noise for RAGAS
metrics reaches $\pm 0.4$, so 20-question per-category deltas below
$\approx 0.05$ are treated as noise (Round~3 measures the floor
directly, Section~\ref{sec:round3}). (ii) RAGAS answer relevancy
applies a ``noncommittal'' rule that zeroes refusals; an honest ``the
provided passages do not state this'' is scored 0, which we found
systematically deflates cautious models (a commercial model's relevancy
rose above the local model's once refusal-affected items were excluded).
(iii) Position and verbosity bias make naive LLM-judged A/B comparisons
overstate differences~\cite{zeng2025unbiased}. (iv) Unit-level recall
is inflated by ranges: a gold ``Matthew 5--7'' (111 verses) is one unit,
so retrieving any one of its verses scores recall 1.0. On the Round~3
graph run, unit-level recall@5 is 0.835 but verse recall@5 0.749 (0.893
vs.\ 0.688 on the original 100); the hit rates and recall@5 values of
Rounds~0--2 are unit-level --- valid as ``some gold passage was
retrieved,'' optimistic about breadth. (v) The faithfulness judge
originally received passage text without the generator's citation
headers (``[1] John, chapter 3 --- \emph{title} (verse 16)''), so the
citations the generator is instructed to write were judged unsupported,
and short, citation-dense answers suffered most: exact-verse questions,
with the best retrieval, scored the lowest faithfulness in every
Round~0--2 run. Re-judging the 500 answers of a July 15, 2026 run with
context blocks rebuilt in the generator's exact format raised
faithfulness from 0.912 to 0.987 (zh) and 0.981 (strict) without
changing a single answer. Every faithfulness value in
Rounds~0--2 predates this repair and is not comparable with Round~3.
Retrieval conclusions in this paper therefore rest on the deterministic
family wherever possible.

Following a July 2026 audit of the instruments, answer coverage is the
primary answer-side metric and faithfulness a gate --- since the
September repair, strict faithfulness $\ge 0.97$ per run and $\ge 0.95$
per question type. Answer correctness is reported
for continuity only: its F1 term penalizes complete answers --- among
Round~3 graph-configuration answers with coverage $\ge 0.8$, answer
length and correctness correlate at $r=-0.45$ --- and it averages only
0.76 on answers whose verse recall ($\ge 0.9$) and coverage
($\ge 0.8$) are both high.

\subsection{A/B Infrastructure and Reproducibility}
\label{sec:evalinfra}

Four properties of the harness matter for the experiments that follow:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Per-request configuration}: \code{use\_graph},
\code{semantic\_only}, and \code{fusion\_alpha} are request-level
overrides, so the graph/no-graph/semantic A/B tests and $\alpha$ sweeps
run against one container without restarts or rebuilds.
\item \textbf{Fast retrieval loop}: with \code{retrieval\_only=true}
the backend skips generation; the 100-question retrieval suite completes
in $\sim$15 minutes versus $\sim$2.5 hours for the full pipeline
(36 min collection $+$ 1\,h\,42\,m RAGAS $+$ 13 min coverage), with
retrieval metrics verified \emph{bit-exact} against the full pipeline
in Rounds~0--2, while the intent classifier was inactive (with a
sampling classifier the two can route a few questions differently).
\item \textbf{Judge-visible context}: with \code{include\_context=true}
the backend returns, for every source, the exact context block the
generator saw, and the judges score against that text --- the repair of
pitfall~(v) above.
\item \textbf{Re-scorable raw outputs}: every run archives raw responses
(sources, strategies, scores per question); metrics can be recomputed
from archives, and a comparison tool emits per-question deltas and
hit-flip lists --- the instrument behind every forensic table in
Sections~\ref{sec:p0}, \ref{sec:fusion}, and~\ref{sec:round3}.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Retriever and encoder caveat.} Two defects found by the
October 2026 audit (Appendix~\ref{app:rebuild}) apply to every round and
remain in the reported numbers. First, retrieval was dense-only
throughout, because the hybrid dense$+$sparse mode never executed
(Section~\ref{sec:strategies}). Second, the serving environment
tokenized with transformers~5.0.0, which skips the NFKC normalization of
the BGE-M3 and reranker tokenizers, whereas the index was built with
version~5.3.0: full-width punctuation became the unknown token, and 499
of the 500 benchmark questions were encoded with input IDs that differ
from the build-side encoding of the same text. The reranker was affected
on both sides of its input --- the query and the passage, whose RCUV
text is punctuated in full width --- and its query--passage pairs also
used a non-standard separator (one end-of-sequence token where the model
expects two). Correcting the serving-side tokenization (the embedder's
query encoding and the reranker's query--passage input), measured in
October 2026 on the 500 questions under that month's serving
configuration (top-5 plus at most one appended passage), changed paired
verse recall@6 by $-0.003$ (95\% CI $[-0.013,+0.006]$) and MRR by
$+0.005$ ($[-0.012,+0.023]$): a correctness fix without measurable
retrieval effect. Since all arms within a round shared the same
encoders, the Round~0--3 comparisons are reported as run.
