\begin{abstract}
Domain-specific question answering (QA) with large language models (LLMs)
faces high API cost, data-privacy constraints, and strict factuality
requirements. We present the complete design, deployment, and four-round
empirical evaluation of a graph-based retrieval-augmented generation
(GraphRAG) system for Traditional Chinese Bible QA, followed by tests of
graph retrieval on the rebuilt system. The legacy system of Rounds~0--3
converted 66 books of the Revised Chinese Union Version from PDF into a
pericope-centric hierarchical corpus by layout-geometry analysis, applied
token-aware hierarchical chunking, and built a knowledge graph with
grounded dual-track entity extraction (9{,}120 entities of six types;
173{,}896 mentions), closed-schema grounded relation extraction (37
relation types), and a 250{,}418-edge cross-reference network that
integrated the Treasury of Scripture Knowledge. Content, vectors, and
graph were stored in PostgreSQL, Qdrant, and Neo4j, and served online by
a signal-driven six-route retrieval architecture with eleven strategies,
cross-encoder reranking, and a linear rank-fusion layer
$s_{\mathrm{fused}}=(1-\alpha)\,s_{\mathrm{rr}}+\alpha\,w$; an October
2026 rebuild retired Neo4j and reduced the knowledge layer to a name
layer (2{,}926 names) and a curated registry of 31 events. On a
100-question benchmark spanning five question types we report a three-round
evidence chain: (i) signal-adaptive retrieval beats a semantic-only
baseline by $+14$--$15$ percentage points hit rate with either answer
model; (ii) a knowledge-graph completeness overhaul (entity anchor coverage
$83.8\%\!\rightarrow\!98.2\%$, cross-references $\times 273$) yields
\emph{zero} retrieval gain on the head-entity benchmark --- a negative
result we diagnose, question by question, to a reranker-monopolized final
ranking, topical cross-reference noise, and benchmark head saturation; and
(iii) a one-day rank-fusion remediation recovers and surpasses all prior
results on the 100 questions it was developed on (hit rate $0.97$,
event-narrative hit rate $1.00$, $+0.16$ over the semantic baseline), with
an ablation showing a complementary structure between discrete data
repairs and continuous score fusion. A fourth round (Round 3)
re-evaluates on 500 questions (the original 100 plus 400 in 19 diagnostic
families) with verse-level metrics, a repaired faithfulness judge, a
repaired intent classifier (silently inactive in all earlier local-model
runs), and a no-graph ablation: routing alone accounts for $+0.13$ of the
replicated $+0.16$ head margin, and the graph's $+0.03$ is not
distinguishable from zero (95\% CI upper bound $+0.08$). On the 399 new questions no
graph gain is detected (verse recall@5 $-0.009$ and answer coverage
$-0.014$, each with upper bound $+0.001$, from one run per configuration);
the direction is consistently negative, but the anchor-coverage@5 deficit
of $-0.012$ has a Holm-adjusted $p=0.107$, and harm survives
multiplicity adjustment only in first-gold-rank win:loss counts (12:35,
sign test $p=0.001$, replicated in a rerun; a statistic chosen after
inspecting the results), a direction we trace to wrongly anchored graph
candidates passing the ranking layer; faithfulness is $\approx 0.98$
throughout. On the rebuilt system, whose knowledge layer has no
entity-identity or relation layer, we tested graph operations over a
name--passage graph --- chiefly personalized PageRank (PPR), used
directly and as name hints for an LLM sub-query writer --- against dense
retrieval that reads the same number of passages ($k$-aligned) and,
for the sub-query arms, against non-graph twins of the same pipeline, on multi-hop questions
written by an LLM and not reviewed by humans. No comparison is
confirmatory, and none detected a gain outside the development set:
dense-seeded PPR, selected on that set, where it won 11 question clusters
and lost none at $k=10$, split 2:2 in a pre-registered check on a second,
partially exposed set and was stopped (this is not evidence of absence);
PPR name hints changed hop coverage by $-0.017$ against their non-graph
twin (upper bound $+0.023$); and apparent gains of graph-hinted
sub-queries were attributable to LLM query rewriting. We relate this
null to reranker selection, missing identity and relation layers, and
little graph-unique headroom, and we stopped graph-retrieval work on
this layer pending a new graph layer and a new pre-registration. We
conclude that, in the legacy rounds,
knowledge-graph construction quality, the retrieval ranking mechanism,
and benchmark design \emph{jointly} determined observable benefit and
that optimization order should follow the bottleneck layer rather than
the upstream-first order of the data flow; and, across both systems, that
a layer's gains must be validated by ablation on questions its repairs
never saw and, for a graph, against both $k$-aligned dense retrieval and
a non-graph twin.
\end{abstract}
