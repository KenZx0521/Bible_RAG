\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Three Co-Determinants of Observable Benefit}

The central methodological claim of this paper is that in a GraphRAG
system, \textbf{knowledge-graph construction quality, the retrieval
ranking mechanism, and benchmark design jointly determine observable
benefit} --- and any one of the three can silently zero out investment
in the others. The evidence chain is summarized by the original
three-round chain (Rounds~0--2) on the legacy system: a completeness
overhaul whose construction metrics all moved (entity anchor coverage
$+14.4$\,pt, event completeness
$+50$\,pt, cross-references $\times 273$) read as \emph{zero} through a
reranker-monopolized ranking onto a head-saturated benchmark
(Section~\ref{sec:p0}); one day of ranking-layer repair then released
more measured value than the week-scale re-extraction program was
projected to deliver on the same benchmark (Section~\ref{sec:fusion})
--- an in-sample comparison, since that repair was developed on the
same 100 questions.
The practical rule we draw: \emph{optimization order should follow the
bottleneck layer, not the upstream-first order of the data flow} ---
and the bottleneck is found by per-question forensics, not by metric
staring.

Round~3 adds the converse. The same co-determinants can also silently
\emph{inflate} a reading: on a head benchmark whose failing graph
anchors had been repaired question by question, the graph was credited
with part of a margin that a no-graph control assigns mostly to signal
routing ($+0.13$ of $+0.16$ on the same 100 questions), while on the 399
new questions no graph gain is detected (answer-coverage upper bound
$+0.001$; Section~\ref{sec:round3}). Benchmark design
therefore decides not only whether a layer's investment is visible but
also to which layer it is credited, and the instrument that settled the
attribution was an ablation removing one layer while holding the others
fixed.

The rebuilt system bounds the claim (Section~\ref{sec:rebuiltgraph}).
The co-determinant frame explains the legacy results, whose positive
graph readings were in-sample, but it does not imply that a graph holds
value which fixing one co-determinant will release. On the rebuilt layer
the benchmark co-determinant was addressed directly --- targeted
multi-hop question sets, $k$-aligned dense controls, non-graph twins, an attribution rule fixed before the October~18 runs (and applied retrospectively to the earlier ones), and pre-registered stopping
rules --- and still no gain was detected. Giving PPR-ranked names to an
LLM decomposer had an upper bound of $+0.023$ in hop coverage (a mean
over question clusters) against its non-graph twin, and adding PPR
candidates to iterative retrieval an upper bound of $+0.027$ (an interval resting on the two untied clusters of 40; both under
a pre-registered stopping rule); dense-seeded PPR alone (GD) had an
upper bound of $+0.054$ at the production $k=5$ (exploratory,
development set). The three failure mechanisms of Section~\ref{sec:rebuiltmech}
touch all three co-determinants at once. (A)~Selection (ranking): the
cross-encoder scores passages only against the question or sub-query,
so, in the per-hop and appended-passage settings, graph-proposed bridge
passages reach the pool but rarely survive to the top-$k$. (B)~No
identity or relation layer (construction): the rebuilt layer has no
entity-identity or relation layer, so names collapse homonyms, and on
the GN-41 questions the legacy graph supplied only 9 of 104 needed
answer-entity groups (3 strictly). (C)~Headroom and evaluation: on NEW82
only about 6 of 102 missing hops appear reachable by the graph and by no
non-graph channel (a count whose script was not archived), the apparent
gains of graph arms with an LLM decomposer over plain dense retrieval
were reproduced by their non-graph twins, and the one positive setting,
chosen on the seen development set, fell from 11:0 to 2:2 on a second
set. Within the limited power of these sets
(Section~\ref{sec:limitations}), the targeted yardstick did not reveal
graph value that the 500-question benchmark had hidden.

Table~\ref{tab:threerounds} aligns the four incidents. The third
deserves elaboration because it was discovered \emph{after} the fusion
remediation and is therefore an independent probe. The two GENERAL
misses remaining after Round~2 are one family: cross-book
\emph{prophecy--fulfillment} questions requiring two anchor sets
simultaneously (GENERAL\_006: Jeremiah's new covenant in Hebrews, gold
$=$ Jer.~31:31--34 $+$ Heb.~8; GENERAL\_008: Zechariah's prophecies in
Passion Week, gold $=$ Zech.~9:9; 11:12--13; 12:10 $+$ Matt.~21/27).
Live verification of the legacy graph showed that \textbf{every needed
cross-reference edge existed} --- Jer.~31$\leftrightarrow$Heb.~8 (3 edges),
Zech.~9$\leftrightarrow$Matt.~21 (2), Zech.~11$\leftrightarrow$Matt.~27
(5), Zech.~12$\leftrightarrow$John~19 (2); TSK is \emph{designed} for
exactly this relation. The failure was upstream of ranking: retrieval
seeds landed in the wrong chapters (``prophecy'' surface forms hit the
false-prophet conflict of Jer.~26/28/29; ``Zechariah'' hit the
Zerubbabel visions of Zech.~4), and 1-hop expansion from wrong seeds
could not reach right edges. Assets in the graph; value gated by the
seed-selection mechanism. These two questions also posted the two
lowest answer-correctness scores of the original suite
($\approx 0.13$, stable across re-judged runs). Round~3 populated the
family (prophecy\_fulfillment, $n=26$), and its reading is consistent
with the diagnosis: with the graph on, hit rate is 0.92 and anchor
coverage@5 0.65, against 0.96 and 0.68 with it off --- expansion from
imperfect seeds adds nothing measurable (edge presence and seed
placement were not re-verified for the new questions).
In Round~3, GENERAL\_008 itself hit, through the entity traversal that
the repaired classifier activated rather than through cross-references.
The planned fix, query decomposition, was later tested on the rebuilt
system in non-graph form, since the rebuilt knowledge layer has no
relation layer and hence no cross-reference edges to join on
(Section~\ref{sec:rebuiltsetup}). On the 204 multi-anchor questions of
GT~v2 (the 500 benchmark questions as evaluated on Rebuild-2),
appending the best passage for one LLM-written sub-query to the same
five passages changed anchor coverage@6 by $+0.0174$ (95\% CI
$[-0.0025, +0.0380]$; question W:L 29:20) relative to appending a
passage from a deeper dense pool. That missed the pre-registered
bar (at least $+0.03$ with a CI lower bound above 0), so the
multi-anchor line was stopped by the pre-registered rule; this is not
evidence of absence (Section~\ref{sec:rebuiltresults}). The dual-anchor
family remains open.

The fourth incident inverted the first two. There, the ranking layer
discarded correct graph evidence; in Round~3 it passed graph evidence
whether or not it was correct, because neither of its signals concerned
the anchor: the reranker scored surface form and the prior scored the
proposing strategy. On the 29 questions where graph strategies took all
five context slots, the graph run trailed the no-graph run by 0.10
verse recall and 0.11 coverage (a descriptive split on the graph run's
own output), and in most graph-caused losses the reranker alone already
preferred the injected passages (Section~\ref{sec:round3mech}).
Injecting graph candidates is a safe default only for verified anchors;
fusion compounds the problem rather than causing it. The rebuild took
the conservative course: apart from routing vocabulary drawn from its
name layer, its only online knowledge-layer signal is a curated event
lookup lane that appends at most one curated anchor after
the top-$k$ instead of injecting candidates into the pool. Even this
lookup, which is not a graph operation, showed a retrieval gain only in
sample, on the 23 of 500 questions where it fired, and switching it on
versus off moved answer coverage by $+0.0089$ (95\% CI
$[-0.0030, +0.0207]$; not $k$-aligned; descriptive;
Section~\ref{sec:rebuiltresults}).

\subsection{Retrieval-to-Answer Attenuation}

Post-fusion, context recall rose $+0.053$ and answer coverage
$+0.041$, while answer correctness moved only $+0.021$ --- at the edge of
judge noise. EVENT\_005 illustrates both limits of reading such numbers.
On the unit-level metrics its retrieval is perfect (context recall
$1.0$, recall@5 $1.0$), yet its answer scored $0.4$ coverage and $0.173$
correctness on the run of record, and re-judging the identical outputs
moved those to $0.70$/$0.353$. The verse-level metrics introduced later
show that the retrieval was not perfect: the gold is Numbers~13--14 and
no archived run retrieved chapter~13, so on the run of record anchor
coverage@5 is 0.5 and verse recall 0.29. And in Round~3 the no-graph and
semantic-only runs received identical contexts for it yet scored 0.7
and 0.4 coverage. Faithfulness is flat across all three time points
($\approx 0.95$) and, under the repaired judge, $\approx 0.98$ in every
Round~3 configuration, which is consistent with the gains coming from
better context; what is lost is shared among retrieval breadth that
unit-level recall hides, the small generation model (Gemma~4 E4B), and
the judged metrics themselves. The quality bottleneck is therefore no
longer retrieval alone --- consistent with our framing that the
observable-benefit chain must be examined end to end. Any answer-model A/B here must first
neutralize the noncommittal-refusal artifact of
Section~\ref{sec:evalmetrics}, or the honest model loses by
construction --- the local instance of the evaluation-bias warnings
in~\cite{zeng2025unbiased}.

\subsection{Benchmark Saturation: Evaluation Design as Prerequisite}

At 97/100 (VERSE, TOPIC, EVENT at 1.00 hit rate), the benchmark is
saturated in the head, and the three residual misses belong to two
families that need \emph{new architecture}, not better ranking:
cross-chapter aggregation (PERSON\_004; the community-summary/RAPTOR
layer~\cite{edge2024graphrag,sarthi2024raptor}) and cross-book
dual-anchor conjunction (GENERAL\_006/008; query decomposition). (Round~3
qualifies both: without the graph PERSON\_004 hits at the unit level,
its graph-mode miss being the displacement of
Section~\ref{sec:round3mech}, although complete coverage still needs
aggregation; and GENERAL\_008 then hit through the classifier-activated
entity traversal.)
Consequently, \emph{every} roadmap item then remaining --- long-tail
re-extraction (P1), event-temporal modeling
(P2,~\cite{lairgi2025atom,zhang2025entityevent,sun2025dygrag}),
hierarchical aggregation (P3) --- would read as zero on this yardstick:
P1's tail improvements are unread by head questions (consistent with
P0, although Round~3's long-tail families show no graph benefit either;
Section~\ref{sec:round3}), P2's target questions already retrieved
perfectly at the unit level (their verse recall is only 0.43; see
below), and P3's target population is $n=1$.
The methodological consequence: \textbf{benchmark redesign is the
prerequisite for all further optimization}, not a reporting
afterthought. The needed instruments have since been built (below and
Section~\ref{sec:rebuiltsetup}); the general principle --- that the benchmark
itself gates which layer's investment is visible --- echoes the
defect-sensitivity methodology of BRINK~\cite{zhou2025brink} and the
task-dependence findings of~\cite{xiang2025whengraphs}. A cautionary
corollary from Section~\ref{sec:fusion}: with $n=2$ family sizes, tuning
the system on the residual misses would be overfitting; the family must
be \emph{populated first}, then fixed.

That prescription has since been carried out. The 500-question benchmark
(Section~\ref{sec:benchmark}) populates the aggregation and dual-anchor
families (trajectory $n=18$, multi\_chapter $n=25$,
prophecy\_fulfillment $n=26$, among 19 families), and its first
reading, with the no-graph control, revised the graph's share of the
margin and showed that the graph's head gains do not transfer to new
questions (Section~\ref{sec:round3}). It also corrects one premise
above: P2's target questions do \emph{not} retrieve perfectly once
breadth is measured --- the temporal family reads verse recall 0.42 and
anchor coverage@5 0.53 under the graph configuration ---
and the aggregation families sit lower still (verse recall 0.34 for
trajectory, 0.37 for multi\_chapter). The yardstick has room again for
multi-anchor retrieval in general, though not enough to detect a graph
gain: only one of the 500 questions has a multi-hop relation as its
primary type and only 15 need two or more hops, and even a perfect
repair of every non-curated question the legacy graph could serve would
have raised verse recall by about $+0.018$ (at most $+0.030$), 1.2--2
times the minimum detectable effect. The 500 questions can reliably
detect graph harm, not a graph gain (Section~\ref{sec:rebuiltsetup}).

For the graph, the prescription was then carried out in October 2026,
mostly on the rebuilt system. Three targeted multi-hop sets were built
--- NEW82 (82 questions, 223 hops), OLD31 (31 questions, 101 hops), and GN-41 (41
questions on the legacy graph) --- and each graph arm was compared with
a $k$-aligned dense control and, where an LLM rewrote the query, with a
non-graph twin that differed only in the graph operation
(Section~\ref{sec:rebuiltsetup}). No graph arm showed a detectable gain
(Table~\ref{tab:rebuiltgraph}) except in one development-set reading:
dense-seeded PPR at $k=10$ (cluster W:L 11:0, selected among about ten
settings; at the production $k=5$ its upper bound is $+0.054$). In the
pre-registered direction check on OLD31 it fell to 2:2 (4 of 20
clusters discordant) and was stopped by the pre-registered rule; this
is not evidence of absence. On the current layer, then, a yardstick
built to see graph value detected none; with these set sizes that
argues against a large hidden gain but cannot exclude a small one.
The principle stands --- a yardstick must be able to see a layer before
that layer's investment is judged --- but a yardstick that can see a
layer may simply report that the layer adds nothing detectable.

\subsection{Patch Lifecycles Under Architecture Evolution}

The pin audit (Section~\ref{sec:fusionlayer}) generalizes: the EQ pin
and graph-uncertainty pin were correct \emph{for the architecture they
were built in} --- discrete rescues under a ranking that discarded
graph signal. Once fusion transmitted that signal continuously, the
same pins became net-negative (noise promotion), and the ``regression''
observed when retiring them (GENERAL NDCG $-0.08$) was itself an
artifact of the artificial placements they used to manufacture.
Remediation machinery must be re-audited whenever the layer it
compensates for changes; yesterday's fix is today's bug. We suspect
this lifecycle is common in deployed RAG systems, where discrete
patches accrete around ranking layers precisely because ranking is the
last mile.

\subsection{Cost and Privacy}

All legacy-round conclusions above were reached with every model of the
system local --- embedder, reranker, extraction classifiers, generator,
and judge --- on a single GPU host; the rebuilt-system experiments kept
the retrieval stack and the decomposer local, but their multi-hop
questions were written, and the OLD31 answers graded, by a commercial
LLM (Claude; Section~\ref{sec:rebuiltsetup}). Round 0 established that a locally served 8-bit E4B-class
model matches a commercial API model end to end on this task within
4\,pp on six of seven metrics~(Table~\ref{tab:round0}). Round~3
qualifies but does not overturn that result --- Gemma's Round~0
classifier was inactive, and its faithfulness values predate the judge
repair --- and an answer-model A/B under the repaired instruments
remains to be run; all optimization value so far has been
retrieval-side. For sensitive religious-domain
deployments --- where both the corpus and user questions may be
confidential --- this is the operative cost/privacy result: the
investment frontier is retrieval and evaluation engineering, not model
procurement.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\textbf{Rebuilt knowledge layer.} The rebuilt system has no
entity-identity or relation layer: its name layer (2{,}926 names) puts
homonyms on one node, and its 31-event registry fires on literal
triggers. On unseen event questions the registry fired on too few
questions (13 of 61) for its pre-registered test, with precision 11/13
and recall 11/43 (descriptive), and 15 of the 23 benchmark questions on
which it fired matched external aliases tuned on the same ground truth.
Of the legacy graph that Rounds~0--3 ran on, the residuals listed in
earlier versions (incomplete pericope-level \textsc{mentions}, alias
coverage of $\sim$0.8\% of entities, unclassified relation candidates,
no coreference resolution, and toneless-pinyin IDs that over-merge
homophones, 266 of them colliding) no longer bear on the deployed
system, which retired that graph.

\textbf{Rebuilt graph experiments.} The graph experiments of
Section~\ref{sec:rebuiltgraph}, none of which is confirmatory, carry
six further limits. (i)~\emph{Development sets.} NEW82 is a seen
development set: the dense-seeded PPR configuration was chosen among
about ten settings on it, and the name formula of the decomposer arms
was revised after inspecting the name lists of six of its questions.
OLD31 is only partially blind: 16 of its 31 questions share gold
passages with NEW82, and the PPR seed type was chosen after its
pool-level results were seen. (ii)~\emph{LLM-authored, unreviewed
sets.} NEW82, OLD31, and GN-41 were authored by an LLM (Claude) and
never reviewed by a human, and the OLD31 answers were graded by two
Claude raters, whose agreement (linear $\kappa$ 0.98) measures
reliability, not validity; three OLD31 questions score zero in every
arm, including the arm given the gold facts, which suggests faulty
items. (iii)~\emph{Low power.} The pre-registered direction check that
stopped dense-seeded PPR would have stopped with probability 0.552
under the null and 0.072 at a cluster win rate of 0.8, so its 2:2
argues against a cluster win rate as high as 0.8 but says little about
moderate or small effects; the OLD31 answer-level comparisons have minimum detectable effects
of 0.085--0.179 at 80\% power; and the 500-question benchmark can
detect graph harm but hardly a graph gain. (iv)~\emph{One generation
per arm.} Round~3 and the GT~v2 comparison of the event lane generated
each answer once, and their question-resampling intervals do not cover
run-level drift (a Rebuild-1 A/A rerun moved answer coverage by
$+0.0149$); the three decompositions averaged by the decomposer arms
are not independent samples. (v)~\emph{Question-level intervals.} The
GD and decomposer-arm intervals resample clusters of questions that
share gold passages, but the OLD31 answer-level, GN-41, GT~v2, and
Round~3 intervals resample questions; OLD31's largest
cluster holds 9 of its 31 questions, so its intervals may be too
narrow. (vi)~\emph{Selection and instruments.} No family-wise control
spans the rebuilt-system program, which ran well over a dozen
comparisons; its positive exploratory readings (GD at $k=10$, QD on
NEW82) carry that multiplicity as well as their selection on the
development set. Mechanism~(A) was observed with one cross-encoder
(BGE-reranker-v2-m3, 512-token input) and the sub-query arms with one
small local LLM (Gemma~4 E4B), so other selectors or decomposers may
behave differently. The count of about 6 graph-only missing hops behind
mechanism~(C) cannot be reproduced, because its script was not
archived. Finally, GD and the October~18 arms were measured at an offline
retrieval stage (host-side exact dense search, without routing or the
event lane) and at the retrieval level only.

\textbf{Benchmark scale and design.} Rounds~0--2 rest on 100
head-entity questions, on which the Round~2 repairs were also developed;
per-category deltas below $\approx 0.05$ on judged metrics are noise
there, and several of their architectural claims rest on question
families of size 1--2, which we deliberately refused to tune against.
Round~3 adds 400 questions in 19 families, but the families are small
(8--48 questions), each configuration was run once, and the ground truth
has known weaknesses: 313 of the 500 items carry exactly five expected
points (a mechanical decomposition of the gold passage), and some points
are broader than what the question asks, so coverage levels are
conservative --- comparisons between configurations, which share the
ground truth, are less affected. The 400 new items were content-checked
in an LLM-assisted review rather than by the annotators of the original
100. The expansion set is diagnostic rather than representative: its
families target known failure modes, some of which favor dense
retrieval and some of which expose graph gaps, so the Round~3 reading
of the graph --- no gain detected, with an upper bound of $+0.001$ on
the expansion answer-coverage difference --- describes this mix, from
one run per configuration. Its harm direction is consistent, but the
mean-difference harm does not survive a Holm adjustment~\cite{holm1979}
over the eight retrieval tests of the later audit (expansion anchor
coverage@5, $-0.012$: raw $p$ 0.013, adjusted 0.107; a retrieval-only
rerun gives $p$ 0.060). Only the first-gold-rank win:loss count survives a Holm adjustment applied to the sign tests of the same eight cells (12:35 on the 399 expansion questions, sign test $p$
0.001), and it replicated in the rerun (13:32, $p$ 0.007); that
statistic was chosen after the results had been seen. Finally, the
semantic-only baseline has no reference parser, so most of the routing gain is the use of a reference that the
user supplied; and the book-anchor change deployed in all Round~3
configurations was itself checked on the 500 questions.

\textbf{Judged metrics.} RAGAS scores carry $\pm 0.4$ per-question
noise at our scale and a noncommittal rule that penalizes honest
refusal; the answer-coverage judge is a single local model, and on
byte-identical contexts coverage still moves by 0.060 per question on
average from generation sampling plus judging (Section~\ref{sec:round3}). Faithfulness values before the
September 2026 judge repair are systematically understated. All
retrieval claims are therefore anchored to deterministic metrics.

\textbf{Configuration history.} In the Gemma configurations of
Rounds~0--2 the intent classifier was silently inactive, so routing was
dictionary-only and the classifier-fed strategies never ran. Round~3 is
the first local-model evaluation with a functioning classifier (Claude's
worked in Round~0). The two regimes meet on the original 100 questions
(graph hit rate 0.97 in both) and on a July~15 run of all 500 questions
(graph-configuration verse recall 0.751 vs.\ 0.749), but the
graph/no-graph contrast exists only under the repaired classifier, whose
re-routing (\eg 48 fallback$\to$R4) increases the graph's exposure. All four rounds also ran dense-only, with a query encoder inconsistent with the index, on a lossy legacy conversion of the corpus (Appendix~\ref{app:rebuild}).

\textbf{Generation.} The deployed generator is an 8-bit E4B-class
model; the attenuation analysis shows it discards part of the retrieval
gains. Conclusions about answer quality are conservative lower bounds.

\textbf{Scope.} One corpus, one language, one translation, one domain.
The architecture's signal set and dictionaries are domain-engineered;
transfer requires re-instantiating those assets (though the
methodological findings --- ranking-layer gating, benchmark dependence,
patch lifecycles --- are domain-agnostic claims about system structure,
not about the Bible). The rebuilt-system null concerns one name-level
knowledge layer over one corpus; it says nothing about graphs that have
identity and relation layers.

\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:future}

\textbf{Evaluation first.} Per Section~\ref{sec:discussion}, the next
artifact was the benchmark, not the pipeline. Done since the first
version of this paper: long-tail question sets, populated
cross-chapter-aggregation and dual-anchor families
(Section~\ref{sec:benchmark}), the faithfulness repair, and, for the
graph, targeted multi-hop sets with $k$-aligned non-graph controls and
pre-registered stopping rules (Section~\ref{sec:rebuiltsetup}).
BRINK-style defect injection~\cite{zhou2025brink} --- remove known
edges, measure answer degradation --- was listed here as a causal probe
of KG value; it presupposes a measurable graph contribution to remove,
and none was detected on the current layer, so it is deferred until a
layer shows a detectable contribution.
Still open: (i) noncommittal exclusion and position/length
debiasing~\cite{zeng2025unbiased}; (ii) an audit of the expected answer
points; (iii) repeated runs per configuration; and (iv) human review of
the LLM-authored multi-hop sets.

\textbf{What reopening the graph line would require.} Graph retrieval
on the current layer is closed (Section~\ref{sec:rebuiltclosure}). It
was measured on the L0 name--passage graph (dense-seeded PPR, GD;
PPR-ranked name hints to an LLM decomposer, QD+G; PPR candidates in
iterative retrieval, IT-GD): GD was stopped by its pre-registered
direction check and QD+G by rule G, and IT-GD, which its own rule did
not stop, was closed with them because the authors' pre-run ruling tied
the closure to the failure of rule G; this is not evidence of absence.
The L1 event registry and kinship surface triples, measured earlier
(the event lookup lane; the kinship walk KIN on OLD31), are closed by
the authors' pre-run ruling. Reopening requires a new graph layer
(\eg entity identity, reliable relations) and a new
pre-registration. In the authors' judgement, a reopened line would
need five things, matched to the mechanisms of
Section~\ref{sec:rebuiltmech}.
(1)~\emph{An entity-identity layer}, so that homonyms stop sharing a
node. The decoupled re-extraction once planned here as P1 is the
obvious route --- whole pericopes as the extraction unit (storage unit
$\ne$ extraction unit~\cite{neo4jbuilder}), a coreference pass
following LINK-KG~\cite{meher2025linkkg}, true-loop
gleanings~\cite{edge2024graphrag,nanographrag}, and post-hoc entity
resolution with propose-then-confirm merging --- but it is no longer
planned work: the rebuild stopped the knowledge layer at a name layer
and an event registry.
(2)~\emph{Reliable relations.} The legacy semantic edges had a strict
precision of only about 0.23--0.28, so a relation layer would need
ontology-grounded correction~\cite{loconte2026betterlater,zhang2024edc},
cross-chunk assembly~\cite{zhang2025rakg,zhang2026crossaug}, and
denoising checked downstream~\cite{zheng2025lessismore}; the
event-temporal layer once planned as
P2~\cite{lairgi2025atom,zhang2025entityevent,sun2025dygrag} is likewise
not planned.
(3)~\emph{A blind, held-out, human-reviewed multi-hop question set},
written before any arm is designed or tuned.
(4)~\emph{Selection that can score bridge passages}: a selector that
judges a passage by the hop it bridges, not only by its match to the
question or sub-query --- for example PPR-ranked passages as in
HippoRAG~2~\cite{gutierrez2025hipporag2} or calibrated graph--vector
fusion~\cite{bacellar2026calibrated}. Plain reciprocal-rank fusion of
reranker and PPR rankings did not help in our one test: in a per-hop
probe on OLD31 it
selected 19 of 58 missing hops, against 21 for the reranker alone
(descriptive).
(5)~\emph{The attribution protocol} of Section~\ref{sec:rebuiltsetup}:
only PPR or a traversal of two or more hops counts as a graph
operation, LLM query rewriting does not, and a graph arm must beat both
a $k$-aligned dense arm and its non-graph twin.

\textbf{Aggregation retrieval.} Community summaries (Leiden
partitions~\cite{traag2019leiden} as in~\cite{edge2024graphrag}),
hierarchical community retrieval~\cite{wang2025archrag}, or RAPTOR-style
summary trees~\cite{sarthi2024raptor} for cross-chapter aggregation
remain untested. They are not retrieval variants on the closed graph
layer, so the closure does not cover them, but they should be judged by
the same protocol. The multi-anchor questions they target remain open:
the non-graph query decomposition aimed at them missed its
pre-registered bar (Section~\ref{sec:rebuiltresults}).

\textbf{Retrieval mechanics.} The retrieval repairs listed here in
earlier versions are no longer open. Anchor-confidence gating, per-strategy
slot quotas, and the $\alpha=0$ run that would have separated injection
from prior effects all concerned graph candidates in the ranked pool,
which the rebuild removed; query decomposition and PPR candidates on
the name layer were tested on the rebuilt system
(Section~\ref{sec:rebuiltgraph}), and graph-native scoring on that
layer --- ranking passages by PPR scores without the cross-encoder, as
HippoRAG~2 does~\cite{gutierrez2025hipporag2} --- falls under the
closure although we did not reproduce that design on this layer; the
closure is a decision about further work, not a result about that
design. The only item those experiments left
open is non-graph: a blind confirmation of LLM sub-query rewriting
(QD). On the NEW82 development set, QD raised hop coverage (cluster-averaged)
over a passage from a deeper dense pool by $+0.101$ (95\% CI
$[+0.058, +0.147]$; cluster W:L 19:2; exploratory, development set);
on the 204 multi-anchor questions of GT~v2 it missed its
pre-registered bar ($+0.0174$, $[-0.0025, +0.0380]$). The blind
confirmation was not run, by decision.

\textbf{Generation.} Answer-model A/B under debiased judging, since the
attenuation analysis locates a further bottleneck there.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented the complete architecture of the GraphRAG system that
served Traditional Chinese Bible QA in Rounds~0--3 --- from
layout-geometric PDF parsing, pericope-centric chunking, grounded dual-track entity extraction,
closed-schema relation extraction, and a three-source cross-reference
network, through a signal-adaptive six-route retrieval design
terminated by cross-encoder reranking and linear rank fusion --- and an
unusually complete empirical record of optimizing it: a quantified
audit tracing KG damage to granularity inheritance rather than chunk
parameters; a completeness overhaul whose construction metrics all
moved and whose benchmark reading was zero; the question-level
diagnosis of that negative result; and a one-day ranking-layer
remediation that surpassed all prior results (hit rate 0.97, EVENT
1.00, $+0.16$ over the semantic baseline), decomposed by ablation into
complementary discrete and continuous mechanisms. A fourth round, on a
500-question benchmark with repaired instruments and a no-graph
control, replicated the head hit rate and margin and re-attributed
them: about four-fifths of the margin over semantic-only belonged to
signal routing, the graph's share ($+0.03$, not distinguishable from
zero; upper bound $+0.08$) was concentrated in head events with
hand-curated anchors, and on 399 new questions no graph gain was
detected (verse recall@5 $-0.009$ and answer coverage $-0.014$, each
with upper bound $+0.001$, from one run per configuration), with wrongly
anchored graph candidates passing a ranking layer that could not tell
them from right ones; the harm direction survived a multiplicity
adjustment only in a first-gold-rank count chosen after the results
were seen. The same round uncovered an intent classifier that had been
silently inactive in every earlier local-model run.

The rebuilt system, whose knowledge layer is reduced to a name layer and
a curated event registry, then gave the graph a targeted test, and graph retrieval showed no detectable gain outside the development set: against $k$-aligned dense controls and, for the decomposer arms, non-graph twins on LLM-authored multi-hop sets, the upper bounds on
the hop-coverage difference (averaged over clusters of questions that
share gold passages) were $+0.054$ for dense-seeded PPR at $k=5$
(exploratory, development set), and $+0.023$ for PPR-ranked name hints
to an LLM decomposer and $+0.027$ for PPR candidates in iterative
retrieval (pre-registered stopping rules), while the apparent gains of
the decomposer arms over plain dense retrieval were reproduced by their
non-graph twins. On the L0 name--passage graph, dense-seeded PPR and the
PPR name hints were stopped by their pre-registered rules. PPR
candidates in iterative retrieval (IT-GD), which their own rule did not
stop ($+0.010$, upper bound $+0.027$), were closed with them by the
authors' pre-run ruling. This is not evidence of absence.
The L1 event registry and kinship surface triples, measured earlier,
are closed by the authors' pre-run ruling, and reopening requires a new
graph layer (\eg entity identity, reliable relations) and a new
pre-registration.

Three conclusions, we believe, carry to other GraphRAG deployments.
First, the measurable gains were not the graph's: signal routing
carried about four-fifths of the Round~3 margin, and on the rebuilt
system the pipeline's gain over
semantic-only ($+0.032$ answer coverage on GT~v2, a development set;
descriptive; Appendix~\ref{app:rebuild}) was concentrated in questions
that cite a reference explicitly ($+0.092$ on 179; $-0.002$, upper
bound $+0.017$, on the other 321). Second,
graph gains were in-sample: the legacy graph's share appeared on head
questions whose anchors had been built or verified on those very
questions and did not transfer to new ones, and on the rebuilt layer
no route of transmission --- the reranker selecting PPR candidates
(apart from a post-hoc $k=10$ reading on NEW82 that fell to 2:2 on
OLD31), PPR names given to an LLM decomposer, or even gold bridge names
(upper bound $+0.008$ over all 82 questions, only 24 of which had
eligible names) --- produced a detectable gain; on NEW82 dense-seeded
PPR at $k=10$ reached only 14 of 102 missing hops that its dense control
missed, and, by a count whose script was not archived, only about 6
were out of reach of every non-graph channel, so there appears to have been
little to transmit. Third, the methods that settled
these readings are portable: establish a layer's contribution by
ablation on questions its repairs never saw; compare a graph arm with a
$k$-aligned dense arm and with a non-graph twin that differs only in
the graph operation; and fix the stopping rule before the run. The
structural observations of the legacy rounds stand alongside: a ranking
layer can hide correct graph evidence (Rounds~1--2) or pass wrong graph
evidence (Round~3); benchmark design decides which layer's investment
is visible, and to which layer it is credited; discrete patches have
architecture-bound lifetimes; and optimization order should follow the
measured bottleneck, not the data flow. In our deployment, the
bottleneck rule delivered, in a day of work, a larger in-sample return
on the head benchmark than the deferred week-scale re-extraction plan
was projected to yield there; the ablation rule then showed that the
graph's share of that return did not transfer beyond the questions it
was built on; and the rebuilt-system experiments found no detectable
graph share to transmit on a name-level layer.
