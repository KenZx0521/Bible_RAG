\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Three Co-Determinants of Observable Benefit}

The central methodological claim of this paper is that in a GraphRAG
system, \textbf{knowledge-graph construction quality, the retrieval
ranking mechanism, and benchmark design jointly determine observable
benefit} --- and any one of the three can silently zero out investment
in the others. The evidence chain is summarized by the original
three-round chain (Rounds~0--2): a completeness overhaul whose
construction metrics all moved (entity anchor coverage $+14.4$\,pt,
event completeness
$+50$\,pt, cross-references $\times 273$) read as \emph{zero} through a
reranker-monopolized ranking onto a head-saturated benchmark
(Section~\ref{sec:p0}); one day of ranking-layer repair then released
more measured value than the week-scale re-extraction program was
projected to deliver on the same benchmark (Section~\ref{sec:fusion}).
The practical rule we draw: \emph{optimization order should follow the
bottleneck layer, not the upstream-first order of the data flow} ---
and the bottleneck is found by per-question forensics, not by metric
staring.

Round~3 adds the converse. The same co-determinants can also silently
\emph{inflate} a reading: on a head benchmark whose failing graph
anchors had been repaired question by question, the graph was credited
with part of a margin that a no-graph control assigns mostly to signal
routing ($+0.13$ of $+0.16$ on the same 100 questions), while on 400 new
questions the graph's share disappears (Section~\ref{sec:round3}). Benchmark design
therefore decides not only whether a layer's investment is visible but
also to which layer it is credited, and the instrument that settled the
attribution was an ablation removing one layer while holding the others
fixed.

Table~\ref{tab:threerounds} aligns the four incidents. The third
deserves elaboration because it was discovered \emph{after} the fusion
remediation and is therefore an independent probe. The two GENERAL
misses remaining after Round~2 are one family: cross-book
\emph{prophecy--fulfillment} questions requiring two anchor sets
simultaneously (GENERAL\_006: Jeremiah's new covenant in Hebrews, gold
$=$ Jer.~31:31--34 $+$ Heb.~8; GENERAL\_008: Zechariah's prophecies in
Passion Week, gold $=$ Zech.~9:9; 11:12--13; 12:10 $+$ Matt.~21/27).
Live graph verification shows \textbf{every needed cross-reference edge
exists} --- Jer.~31$\leftrightarrow$Heb.~8 (3 edges),
Zech.~9$\leftrightarrow$Matt.~21 (2), Zech.~11$\leftrightarrow$Matt.~27
(5), Zech.~12$\leftrightarrow$John~19 (2); TSK is \emph{designed} for
exactly this relation. The failure is upstream of ranking: retrieval
seeds land in the wrong chapters (``prophecy'' surface forms hit the
false-prophet conflict of Jer.~26/28/29; ``Zechariah'' hits the
Zerubbabel visions of Zech.~4), and 1-hop expansion from wrong seeds
cannot reach right edges. Assets in the graph; value gated by the
seed-selection mechanism. These two questions also post the two
lowest answer-correctness scores of the original suite
($\approx 0.13$, stable across re-judged runs). Round~3 populated the
family (prophecy\_fulfillment, $n=26$), and its reading is consistent
with the diagnosis: with the graph on, hit rate is 0.92 and anchor
coverage@5 0.65, against 0.96 and 0.68 with it off --- expansion from
imperfect seeds adds nothing measurable (edge presence and seed
placement were not re-verified for the new questions).
GENERAL\_008 itself now hits, through the entity traversal that the
repaired classifier activated rather than through cross-references.

The fourth incident inverts the first two. There, the ranking layer
discarded correct graph evidence; in Round~3 it passes graph evidence
whether or not it is correct, because neither of its signals concerns
the anchor: the reranker scores surface form and the prior scores the
proposing strategy. On the 29 questions where graph strategies took all
five context slots, the graph run trailed the no-graph run by 0.10
verse recall and 0.11 coverage (a descriptive split on the graph run's
own output), and in most graph-caused losses the reranker alone already
preferred the injected passages (Section~\ref{sec:round3mech}).
Injecting graph candidates is a safe default only for verified anchors;
fusion compounds the problem rather than causing it.

\subsection{Retrieval-to-Answer Attenuation}

Post-fusion, context recall rose $+0.053$ and answer coverage
$+0.041$, while answer correctness moved only $+0.021$ --- at the edge of
judge noise. EVENT\_005 illustrates both limits of reading such numbers.
On the unit-level metrics its retrieval is perfect (context recall
$1.0$, recall@5 $1.0$), yet its answer scored $0.4$ coverage and $0.173$
correctness on the run of record, and re-judging the identical outputs
moved those to $0.70$/$0.353$. The verse-level metrics introduced later
show that the retrieval was not perfect: the gold is Numbers~13--14 and
no archived run retrieved chapter~13, so on the run of record anchor
coverage@5 is 0.5 and verse recall 0.29. And in Round~3 the no-graph and
semantic-only runs received identical contexts for it yet scored 0.7
and 0.4 coverage. Faithfulness is flat across all three time points
($\approx 0.95$) and, under the repaired judge, $\approx 0.98$ in every
Round~3 configuration, which is consistent with the gains coming from
better context; what is lost is shared among retrieval breadth that
unit-level recall hides, the small generation model (Gemma~4 E4B), and
the judged metrics themselves. The quality bottleneck is therefore no
longer retrieval alone --- consistent with our framing that the
observable-benefit chain must be examined end to end. Any answer-model A/B here must first
neutralize the noncommittal-refusal artifact of
Section~\ref{sec:evalmetrics}, or the honest model loses by
construction --- the local instance of the evaluation-bias warnings
in~\cite{zeng2025unbiased}.

\subsection{Benchmark Saturation: Evaluation Design as Prerequisite}

At 97/100 (VERSE, TOPIC, EVENT at 1.00 hit rate), the benchmark is
saturated in the head, and the three residual misses belong to two
families that need \emph{new architecture}, not better ranking:
cross-chapter aggregation (PERSON\_004; the community-summary/RAPTOR
layer~\cite{edge2024graphrag,sarthi2024raptor}) and cross-book
dual-anchor conjunction (GENERAL\_006/008; query decomposition). (Round~3
qualifies both: without the graph PERSON\_004 hits at the unit level,
its graph-mode miss being the displacement of
Section~\ref{sec:round3mech}, although complete coverage still needs
aggregation; and GENERAL\_008 now hits through the classifier-activated
entity traversal.)
Consequently, \emph{every} remaining roadmap item --- long-tail
re-extraction (P1), event-temporal modeling
(P2,~\cite{lairgi2025atom,zhang2025entityevent,sun2025dygrag}),
hierarchical aggregation (P3) --- would read as zero on this yardstick:
P1's tail improvements are unread by head questions (consistent with
P0, although Round~3's long-tail families show no graph benefit either;
Section~\ref{sec:round3}), P2's target questions already retrieved
perfectly at the unit level (their verse recall is only 0.43; see
below), and P3's target population is $n=1$.
The methodological consequence: \textbf{benchmark redesign is the
prerequisite for all further optimization}, not a reporting
afterthought. We specify the needed instruments in
Section~\ref{sec:future}; the general principle --- that the benchmark
itself gates which layer's investment is visible --- echoes the
defect-sensitivity methodology of BRINK~\cite{zhou2025brink} and the
task-dependence findings of~\cite{xiang2025whengraphs}. A cautionary
corollary from Section~\ref{sec:fusion}: with $n=2$ family sizes, tuning
the system on the residual misses would be overfitting; the family must
be \emph{populated first}, then fixed.

That prescription has since been carried out. The 500-question benchmark
(Section~\ref{sec:benchmark}) populates the aggregation and dual-anchor
families (trajectory $n=18$, multi\_chapter $n=25$,
prophecy\_fulfillment $n=26$, among 19 families), and its first
reading, with the no-graph control, revised the graph's share of the
margin and showed that the graph's head gains do not transfer to new
questions (Section~\ref{sec:round3}). It also corrects one premise
above: P2's target questions do \emph{not} retrieve perfectly once
breadth is measured --- the temporal family reads verse recall 0.42 and
anchor coverage@5 0.53 under the graph configuration ---
and the aggregation families sit lower still (verse recall 0.34 for
trajectory, 0.37 for multi\_chapter). The yardstick has room again.

\subsection{Patch Lifecycles Under Architecture Evolution}

The pin audit (Section~\ref{sec:fusionlayer}) generalizes: the EQ pin
and graph-uncertainty pin were correct \emph{for the architecture they
were built in} --- discrete rescues under a ranking that discarded
graph signal. Once fusion transmitted that signal continuously, the
same pins became net-negative (noise promotion), and the ``regression''
observed when retiring them (GENERAL NDCG $-0.08$) was itself an
artifact of the artificial placements they used to manufacture.
Remediation machinery must be re-audited whenever the layer it
compensates for changes; yesterday's fix is today's bug. We suspect
this lifecycle is common in deployed RAG systems, where discrete
patches accrete around ranking layers precisely because ranking is the
last mile.

\subsection{Cost and Privacy}

All conclusions above were reached with every model local --- embedder,
reranker, extraction classifiers, generator, and judge --- on a single
GPU host. Round 0 established that a locally served 8-bit E4B-class
model matches a commercial API model end to end on this task within
4\,pp on six of seven metrics~(Table~\ref{tab:round0}). Round~3
qualifies but does not overturn that result --- Gemma's Round~0
classifier was inactive, and its faithfulness values predate the judge
repair --- and an answer-model A/B under the repaired instruments
remains to be run; all optimization value so far has been
retrieval-side. For sensitive religious-domain
deployments --- where both the corpus and user questions may be
confidential --- this is the operative cost/privacy result: the
investment frontier is retrieval and evaluation engineering, not model
procurement.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\textbf{Knowledge-graph residuals.} 164 chunk-only entities still lack
pericope-level \textsc{mentions} in the graph itself (retrieval-side
remap restores their visibility, the graph remains incomplete); alias
coverage is $\sim$0.8\% of entities (dictionary and curated sources are
exhausted; mass production requires entity-resolution merge-back);
67{,}306 relation candidates remain unclassified (the 77{,}953-pair
pile minus the 10{,}647 Event-related pairs consumed or set aside by
the P0 rescue); the event layer has
only 26 native temporal/causal Event--Event edges live (the audit-time
figure of 45 included miscounted person-succession edges and edges of
since-deleted generic events) plus 277 unrescued Event--Event
candidates; there is no coreference resolution; toneless-pinyin IDs can
over-merge homophones; and duplicate-name splits (\eg Israel as
Place vs.\ Israelites as Group) await entity resolution.

\textbf{Benchmark scale and design.} Rounds~0--2 rest on 100
head-entity questions, on which the Round~2 repairs were also developed;
per-category deltas below $\approx 0.05$ on judged metrics are noise
there, and several of their architectural claims rest on question
families of size 1--2, which we deliberately refused to tune against.
Round~3 adds 400 questions in 19 families, but the families are small
(8--48 questions), each configuration was run once, and the ground truth
has known weaknesses: 313 of the 500 items carry exactly five expected
points (a mechanical decomposition of the gold passage), and some points
are broader than what the question asks, so coverage levels are
conservative --- comparisons between configurations, which share the
ground truth, are less affected. The 400 new items were content-checked
in an LLM-assisted review rather than by the annotators of the original
100. The expansion set is diagnostic rather than representative: its
families target known failure modes, some of which favor dense
retrieval and some of which expose graph gaps, so ``neutral to slightly
harmful'' describes the graph on this mix, from one run per
configuration. Finally, the semantic-only baseline has no reference
parser, so most of the routing gain is the use of a reference that the
user supplied; and the book-anchor change deployed in all Round~3
configurations was itself checked on the 500 questions.

\textbf{Judged metrics.} RAGAS scores carry $\pm 0.4$ per-question
noise at our scale and a noncommittal rule that penalizes honest
refusal; the answer-coverage judge is a single local model, and on
byte-identical contexts coverage still moves by 0.060 per question on
average from generation sampling plus judging (Section~\ref{sec:round3}). Faithfulness values before the
September 2026 judge repair are systematically understated. All
retrieval claims are therefore anchored to deterministic metrics.

\textbf{Configuration history.} In the Gemma configurations of
Rounds~0--2 the intent classifier was silently inactive, so routing was
dictionary-only and the classifier-fed strategies never ran. Round~3 is
the first local-model evaluation with a functioning classifier (Claude's
worked in Round~0). The two regimes meet on the original 100 questions
(graph hit rate 0.97 in both) and on a July~15 run of all 500 questions
(graph-configuration verse recall 0.751 vs.\ 0.749), but the
graph/no-graph contrast exists only under the repaired classifier, whose
re-routing (\eg 48 fallback$\to$R4) increases the graph's exposure. All four rounds also ran dense-only, with a query encoder inconsistent with the index, on a lossy legacy conversion of the corpus (Appendix~\ref{app:rebuild}).

\textbf{Generation.} The deployed generator is an 8-bit E4B-class
model; the attenuation analysis shows it discards part of the retrieval
gains. Conclusions about answer quality are conservative lower bounds.

\textbf{Scope.} One corpus, one language, one translation, one domain.
The architecture's signal set and dictionaries are domain-engineered;
transfer requires re-instantiating those assets (though the
methodological findings --- ranking-layer gating, benchmark dependence,
patch lifecycles --- are domain-agnostic claims about system structure,
not about the Bible).

\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:future}

\textbf{Evaluation first.} Per Section~\ref{sec:discussion}, the next
artifact was the benchmark, not the pipeline. Done since the first
version of this paper: long-tail question sets, populated
cross-chapter-aggregation and dual-anchor families
(Section~\ref{sec:benchmark}), and the faithfulness repair. Still open:
(i) BRINK-style defect injection~\cite{zhou2025brink} --- remove known
edges, measure answer degradation --- as a causal probe of KG value that
no head benchmark can fake, made more pressing by Round~3, since a
no-graph ablation shows whether the graph helps on average but not
which edges carry the value; (ii) noncommittal exclusion and
position/length debiasing~\cite{zeng2025unbiased}; (iii) an audit of the
expected answer points; and (iv) repeated runs per configuration.

\textbf{P1 --- decoupled re-extraction.} Feed extraction whole
pericopes (storage unit $\ne$ extraction unit~\cite{neo4jbuilder});
add a coreference pass scoped to pericope$+$chapter context following
the LINK-KG recipe~\cite{meher2025linkkg}; adopt true-loop gleanings as
in the GraphRAG lineage~\cite{edge2024graphrag} (\eg the nano-graphrag
implementation~\cite{nanographrag}); post-hoc entity resolution with
lexical$+$embedding double signals and propose-then-confirm merging,
writing merged surface forms back as aliases (the alias-generation
mechanism identified in the cross-system survey); type assignment by
majority vote; description regeneration by merge-and-summarize
(cf.~LightRAG~\cite{guo2024lightrag}); ontology-grounded
post-correction as the cheap alternative to
re-extraction~\cite{loconte2026betterlater,zhang2024edc}; and KG
denoising with downstream verification~\cite{zheng2025lessismore}.

\textbf{P2 --- the event-temporal layer.} Promote the event layer from
participants/places (now 84\%/75.5\%) to temporal--causal structure,
following atomic-fact dual-time construction~\cite{lairgi2025atom} and
entity--event dual-graph designs~\cite{zhang2025entityevent,sun2025dygrag};
the 77{,}953-pair provenance-kept rejection pile and the 277 unrescued
Event--Event candidates are the ready inputs.

\textbf{P3 --- aggregation retrieval.} Community summaries (Leiden
partitions~\cite{traag2019leiden} as in~\cite{edge2024graphrag}) or
RAPTOR-style summary trees~\cite{sarthi2024raptor} for
cross-chapter aggregation; RAKG-style pre-entity
re-retrieval~\cite{zhang2025rakg} and cross-chunk graph
augmentation~\cite{zhang2026crossaug} for cross-boundary relations.

\textbf{Retrieval mechanics.} Anchor-confidence gating, the repair
Round~3 points to: admit or weight graph candidates by the confidence of
their entity resolution, with penalties for hub entities and generic
events --- since most wrong anchors are injected rather than merely
up-weighted, the gate must act on candidates, not only on the prior;
per-strategy slot quotas in the top-5; a re-sweep of $\alpha$ on the
500 questions (an $\alpha=0$ run also separates injection from prior
effects); query decomposition for dual-anchor conjunctions
(retrieve prophecy and fulfillment sides separately, then join on
cross-reference edges); theme-level anchoring; and, if the
strategy-prior fusion reaches its ceiling, calibrated score fusion in
the spirit of~\cite{bacellar2026calibrated} or graph-native retrieval
scoring~\cite{gutierrez2025hipporag2,wang2025archrag}.

\textbf{Generation.} Answer-model A/B under debiased judging, since the
attenuation analysis locates a further bottleneck there.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented the complete architecture of a deployed GraphRAG system
for Traditional Chinese Bible QA --- from layout-geometric PDF parsing,
pericope-centric chunking, grounded dual-track entity extraction,
closed-schema relation extraction, and a three-source cross-reference
network, through a signal-adaptive six-route retrieval design
terminated by cross-encoder reranking and linear rank fusion --- and an
unusually complete empirical record of optimizing it: a quantified
audit tracing KG damage to granularity inheritance rather than chunk
parameters; a completeness overhaul whose construction metrics all
moved and whose benchmark reading was zero; the question-level
diagnosis of that negative result; and a one-day ranking-layer
remediation that surpassed all prior results (hit rate 0.97, EVENT
1.00, $+0.16$ over the semantic baseline), decomposed by ablation into
complementary discrete and continuous mechanisms. A fourth round, on a
500-question benchmark with repaired instruments and a no-graph
control, replicated the head hit rate and margin and re-attributed
them: about four-fifths of the margin over semantic-only belongs to
signal routing, the graph's share is concentrated in head events with
hand-curated anchors, and on 400 new questions the graph is neutral to
slightly harmful, because wrongly anchored graph candidates pass a
ranking layer that cannot tell them from right ones. The same round
uncovered an intent classifier that had been silently inactive in every
earlier local-model run.

The system-level conclusions are, we believe, portable to any GraphRAG
deployment. Graph assets have no value until the ranking layer
transmits them, and a ranking layer that cannot judge anchors transmits
wrong ones too; benchmark design decides which layer's investment is
visible at all, and to which layer it is credited; discrete patches
have architecture-bound lifetimes; a layer's contribution is established
only by an ablation on questions its repairs never saw; and the correct
optimization order follows the measured bottleneck, not the data flow.
In our deployment, the bottleneck rule delivered, in a day of work, a
larger measured return on the head benchmark than the deferred
week-scale re-extraction plan was projected to yield there; the
ablation rule, applied in Round~3, then showed that the graph's share of
that return does not transfer beyond the questions it was built on ---
and identified the next bottlenecks (anchor precision in the ranking
layer, then generation) before any further construction spend.
