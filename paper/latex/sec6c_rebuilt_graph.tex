\section{Graph Retrieval on the Rebuilt System: No Gain Detected}
\label{sec:rebuiltgraph}

The October 2026 rebuild, which followed a data audit, retired the legacy
graph of Sections~\ref{sec:kg}--\ref{sec:round3} rather than rebuilding it
(Appendix~\ref{app:rebuild}). We then tested whether graph operations
that can be built on the rebuilt knowledge layer improve multi-hop
retrieval, pairing each graph arm with a non-graph arm that reads the same
number of passages ($k$-aligned). None showed a detectable gain outside
the development set, at the production $k$, or against a non-graph twin:
the one positive reading, dense-seeded PPR at $k=10$ on the set on which
it was selected, is post hoc and did not hold on a second set. This
section therefore reports upper bounds and three mechanisms consistent
with the null. No comparison is
confirmatory: the question sets are small, written by an LLM, and partly
used during development.

\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:rebuiltsetup}

\textbf{Rebuilt knowledge layer.} There are two rebuilt releases,
Rebuild-1 (\code{b20261008\_6daa4f31}) and Rebuild-2
(\code{b20261008\_e05d3e55}); all rebuilt-system experiments below use
the data layer of Rebuild-2. Neither has Neo4j, an entity-identity layer, or a relation
layer. What remains is the L0 name layer (2{,}926 names) and the L1
curated event registry (31 events, 33 in Rebuild-1, with 179 hand-curated
anchors); the query-alias table has 17 entries. Online, the name layer
serves only as routing vocabulary for person and place signals, and the
registry enters retrieval only as a \emph{curated event lookup lane}: a
literal trigger match that appends
at most one anchor passage after the finished top-$k$, takes no part in
ranking, and picks the first anchor the core lacks, regardless of what
the question asks. It is a lookup, not a graph operation.

\textbf{Graph and retrieval stage.} The PPR arms use an unweighted
bipartite graph linking the 2{,}773 passages to 2{,}929 body-text name
keys by 16{,}616 edges; 635 passages carry no name. The keys are the
normalized proper-name spans of the passage bodies (hence 2{,}929, not
the 2{,}926 names of L0), so different people with the same name share a
node. GD and the October~18 arms run offline at the retrieval stage: exact float64
cosine over the 34{,}058 BGE-M3 records (a passage takes its best
record's score), then the production cross-encoder (BGE-reranker-v2-m3,
whole passage, maximum length 512), which scores each candidate against
the question or, in the sub-query arms, against the sub-query. Routing,
book anchoring, and the event lane are not involved, so absolute levels
are not production levels.

\textbf{Arms} (names as in Table~\ref{tab:rebuiltgraph}).
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{GD} (our label for single-shot, dense-seeded PPR; October 14--15). The
pool is the dense top-20 plus the 10 highest-PPR passages outside it,
with PPR seeded by the dense top-10 (weights $1/\mathrm{rank}$, restart
probability 0.5, 40 iterations); the control C fills the same 30-passage
pool with dense ranks 21--30. The cross-encoder picks the top-$k$
($k=10$ primary, $k=5$ the production $k$). PPR is the operator family
of HippoRAG~2~\cite{gutierrez2025hipporag2}; GD does not reproduce that
system.
\item \textbf{Appended-passage arms} (``realkg''; OLD31, answer level). Every arm keeps the production
top-5 (D5) and appends $m\ge 1$ passages per question (49 in total; KIN
and L0 append fewer where they have too few candidates); the
$k$-aligned dense control DK is production retrieval with top-$k=5+m$. KIN
walks 1--2 hops over name-level kinship triples that a prototype
extracted from the rebuilt text (not a production layer); L0 takes the
passages whose body names include a name linked in the question; both
rerank against the question. ORACLE appends gold passages for the missed
hops and FACTS gives the generator an author-written fact chain; both
are ceilings, not methods.
\item \textbf{Iterative arms} (``iterq''; OLD31, answer level). At each of $m$ steps Gemma~4 E4B
writes a sub-query $s_t$ from the question and the current context, and
one passage is appended: IT-D draws it from the dense candidates of
$s_t$, IT-L0 from the L0 passages of the names linked in $s_t$.
\item \textbf{Sub-query arms of October~18} (NEW82, retrieval). Every arm starts from
C5, the five passages C ranks first, and appends one passage outside C5
($k=6$; C6 and C7 are C's top six and seven). DEEP adds the
cross-encoder's best passage for the question among its dense top-50
outside C5, with no LLM. QD (query decomposition) has Gemma~4 E4B (temperature 0) write one sub-query from
the question and C5 and adds the cross-encoder's best dense candidate for it. QD+G adds
to the same prompt a line of five names ranked by PPR on the L0 graph
seeded with the C5 passages; its non-graph twin QD+N uses the same
template and name formula on the question's dense ranking; QD+ORACLE
inserts eligible gold bridge names (a diagnostic). QD-type arms average
three decompositions. Here IT-D (unlike the OLD31 arm of the same name)
runs two context-conditioned sub-query steps
after C5 ($k=7$); IT-GD adds PPR candidates at each step, seeded with the
dense top-10 of the current sub-query $s_t$, not with C5.
\item \textbf{GN-41} (legacy Neo4j KG, context). KG bridge names added to
the query, and KG facts given to the generator, each against the same
pipeline's LLM query rewrite (top-5).
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Attribution rule.} Written into the pre-registration of the
October 18 run before it started; for the earlier experiments (GD,
realkg, iterq, and the event lane) we apply it retrospectively, after
their results were known. Under it: only PPR or a traversal of two or more hops counts as
a graph operation; a one-hop lookup is descriptive; LLM query rewriting
is not a graph operation; and a graph arm must beat both the $k$-aligned
dense arm and its non-graph twin. GD, KIN, QD+G, and IT-GD are graph
operations; L0, IT-L0, and the event lane are lookups. Its main
retrospective effect is to class the event lane's in-sample,
$k$-aligned retrieval gain on the 23 questions where it fired
(Section~\ref{sec:rebuiltresults}) as a lookup result, not as graph
evidence.

\textbf{Question sets.} \emph{NEW82}: 82 multi-hop questions with 223
hops (36, 34, 11, and 1 questions of two to five hops) in eight
categories, kept from 90 LLM drafts. Against a host-side approximation of
the production top-5, 102 hops in 63 questions are missing. Questions
that share a gold passage form union-find clusters: 40, the largest with
12 questions. NEW82 is the development set (GD was chosen on it), so
every result on it is exploratory. \emph{OLD31}: the 31 of 38 earlier
LLM-written questions on which frozen production top-5 retrieval missed
at least one hop; 101 hops, 58 missing, 20 clusters (largest 9).
Selected for production failure, its baselines are low by construction,
and it is only partially blind: 16 of its questions share gold passages
with NEW82, GD's seed form was chosen after OLD31 pool results, and
realkg, iterq, and the per-hop probes had used it. \emph{GN-41}: 41
questions on the legacy KG (13 title-bridge, 11 disambiguation, 8
kinship two-hop, 9 aggregation). \emph{GT~v2}: the 500 benchmark
questions of Section~\ref{sec:benchmark} as evaluated on Rebuild-2; 204
have more than one reference (\emph{multi-anchor}). This subset had
already been analyzed on the rebuilt system before the multi-anchor
sub-query step was designed, and that step used a host-side dense
reimplementation, not production retrieval.

\textbf{Why not the 500-question benchmark.} It is powered to detect
broad harm from a graph, not the small gains a graph could add on it.
275 questions (55\%) state the answer location (167 explicit citations,
108 single-anchor lexical lookups); after an LLM-assisted relabeling one
question has multi-hop as its primary type and 15 need two or more hops;
and if the legacy KG had served perfectly every non-curated question it
could, the ideal verse-recall gain ($+0.018$, at most $+0.030$) would be
only 1.2--2 times its minimum detectable effect (0.0146), and any
realistic repair would deliver a fraction of that ideal.

\textbf{Metrics and statistics.} Retrieval is scored by hop coverage
$f(q)=\frac{1}{H}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\mathbf{1}[\mathrm{gold}_h\cap
\mathrm{top}\text{-}k\neq\emptyset]$ for a question with $H$ hops. OLD31
answers are graded blind by two Claude raters and averaged over raters
and three generations (linear $\kappa$ 0.98, which measures agreement,
not validity); GN-41 uses a deterministic answer-key score and GT~v2 the
metrics of Section~\ref{sec:evalmetrics}. On NEW82, and for GD on OLD31,
the unit is the cluster: $\bar{\Delta}$ is the mean of the clusters' mean
paired differences (zeros included), W:L counts clusters with positive
and negative means, the test is an exact two-sided cluster sign-flip, and
the 95\% CI is a percentile bootstrap over clusters (10{,}000 resamples,
seed 20261014). realkg, iterq, GN-41, GT~v2, and Round~3 use questions
as the unit (paired bootstrap, 10{,}000 resamples), with sign and exact
Wilcoxon tests (realkg, iterq), a bootstrap $p$ (GN-41), or sign-flip and sign tests (the three-arm GT~v2 run, Round~3), and Holm's adjustment~\cite{holm1979} where
a family was declared. The \emph{upper bound} is the upper end of the 95\% CI.

\textbf{Evidence classes.} Nothing here is confirmatory. \emph{Pre-registered
stopping rule}: GD's first pre-registered direction check on OLD31 (V1),
rules G, O, and I of October 18, and the multi-anchor QD bar of its
step~2, each committed to the project repository before its data were
read (commits in Appendix~\ref{app:repro}; no external registry); a stop
is ``stopped by the pre-registered rule; this is not
evidence of absence'', and since rules G, O, and I ran on the development
set, a pass would have been exploratory only. \emph{Exploratory
(development set)}: everything on NEW82, realkg and iterq on OLD31, and
GN-41, whose stop rule was written after generation. \emph{Descriptive}:
the event lookup lane, the release comparison, and the per-hop probes.
Legacy Round~3 was not pre-registered.

\textbf{Who wrote the questions.} NEW82, OLD31, and GN-41 were written by
an LLM (Claude) and never reviewed by a human. An independent LLM audit
of NEW82 rated 33 questions ok, 33 minor, 11 major, and 5 critical before
the flagged items were revised; three
OLD31 questions score 0 in all six realkg arms, FACTS included, which
suggests defective items; and the OLD31 answers were graded by two Claude
raters. This limits external validity (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).

\textbf{Single-shot PPR (GD).} On NEW82 at $k=10$, GD beat C in all 11
discordant clusters ($+0.063$ $[+0.028, +0.104]$, exact $p=1/1024$), but
GD was selected after the fact from about ten settings on this set, so we
do not read that interval as a bound. At the production $k=5$ no gain was
detected (8:2 clusters, upper bound $+0.054$), and against a pool of the
dense top-20 alone GD reads $+0.005$ $[-0.026, +0.034]$. The
pre-registered direction check V1 on OLD31 found 2:2 with 4 of 20 clusters
discordant: stopped by the pre-registered rule; this is not evidence of
absence. At the discordance rate assumed in the pre-registration (0.222)
the rule stops with probability 0.552 under the null, 0.190 at a cluster win rate of 0.7, and 0.072 at
0.8, so the stop argues against a cluster win rate as high as 0.8 but
says little about moderate or small effects. The blind
held-out set (120 questions) was never generated, the confirmatory
comparison never ran, and the answer layer was never measured.

\textbf{Kinship walk and name lookups (OLD31).} KIN and L0 tie with DK
on most questions (W:T:L 3:26:2 and 4:24:3); the effects detectable with
80\% power are 0.085 and 0.121. In the iterative pipeline the name-layer
pool reads $-0.105$ against the dense pool (3:18:10, sign $p=0.092$); its
pre-coded rule did not judge IT-L0 worse, so we read it as no gain
detected, direction negative. The answer-level headroom is large,
although by mechanism~(C) little of it is reachable only through the
graph: from DK's mean of
0.134, gold passages raise correctness to 0.481 and a full fact chain to
0.637, whereas KIN and L0 reach 0.129 and 0.151.

\textbf{PPR names for an LLM decomposer (October 18).} QD+G beat DEEP
(16:2 clusters, $+0.082$ $[+0.038, +0.131]$), but so did its non-graph twin
(QD+N, 18:2, $+0.099$ $[+0.050, +0.150]$). Rule G required QD+G to pass a
gate (cluster W $\ge$ 2L, W$-$L $\ge$ 4, and $\bar{\Delta}>0$) against both QD+N and DEEP, and
to beat plain QD on $\bar{\Delta}$ and W:L; it lost to QD+N 6:8 and to QD 6:10. Rule G failed:
stopped by the pre-registered rule; this is not evidence of absence. The
6:8 is not a near miss: with 12--16 discordant clusters the gate passes
with probability 0.92--0.93 at a cluster win rate of 0.8, against
0.11--0.19 under the null. Gold bridge names did not help QD either (rule
O: 5:4), although only 24 of 82 questions had eligible names, and adding
PPR candidates to iterative retrieval gave $+0.010$ with 2:0 clusters
(rule I; 38 of 40 clusters tied, so the interval $[0.000, +0.027]$ rests
on two clusters), with identical final contexts on 72 of 82 questions.
Rule I did not itself stop IT-GD; the authors' pre-run ruling specified
that a failure of rule G closes every retrieval variant on the current
graph layer, IT-GD included (Section~\ref{sec:rebuiltclosure}). Mean
$f(q)$@6 was 0.685
for QD, 0.665 for QD+G, 0.684 for QD+N, and 0.581 for DEEP. No arm used
a decomposer fallback (0 of 246 decompositions each), and the
cross-encoder scores of 32 fixed query--passage reference pairs
reproduced exactly before the run and after each step.

\textbf{Lookups and releases (GT~v2).} In the three-arm GT~v2 run
(full pipeline, the same without the event lane, and semantic-only;
``eval3c'' in the artifacts), switching the event lookup lane on moved
answer coverage by $+0.0089$ (Holm $p=0.137$ over that run's three
primary comparisons). The lane fired on 23 questions, and contexts were
identical on 473 of 500 questions; with one generation per
arm, most changed per-question outcomes therefore reflect answer noise,
not the lane. The comparison is not $k$-aligned (fired questions read six
passages, not five). On the 23 the change is $+0.053$
$[-0.040, +0.163]$, against an observed minimum detectable effect of 0.155;
a $k$-aligned retrieval check on them (verse recall $+0.163$
$[+0.069, +0.266]$) is in-sample and measures a lookup, not a graph. On
NEW82 the lane fired once. Between releases, top-$k$ changed on 24 of 500
questions (4 in an A/A rerun), and answer coverage on 200 questions moved
by $+0.0019$ $[-0.0138, +0.0172]$, less than the A/A drift of $+0.0149$.

\textbf{Legacy graph.} Under multiplicity correction, Round~3 detected no
gain (Section~\ref{sec:round3}): the expansion anchor-coverage
loss ($-0.012$, raw $p=0.013$) has Holm-adjusted $p=0.107$ over the
eight-test family, and $p=0.060$ in a retrieval-only rerun. The harm
direction is consistent, but only the first-gold-rank win:loss counts
survive a Holm adjustment applied to the sign tests of the same eight
cells (MRR 12:35, sign $p=0.001$), with the same direction in the rerun
(13:32, $p=0.007$; the rerun is not part of that family), and that
statistic was chosen after looking. On GN-41
neither KG arm beat the LLM rewrite (Holm $p=1$ for both) on a set near
saturation (no-graph key score 0.866); its stop rule was written after
generation, so the result is a null, not a pre-registered stop.

\textbf{Non-graph context.} The largest retrieval gain on NEW82 is LLM
sub-query rewriting without a graph (QD $-$ DEEP, 19:2 clusters). It comes
from the rewritten query, not from the extra slot: QD $-$ C6 is $+0.099$,
DEEP $-$ C6 $-0.002$ $[-0.017, +0.015]$. It did not carry over to the 204
multi-anchor GT~v2 questions (29:20), which missed the bar of $\ge +0.03$
with a CI lower bound above zero: stopped by the pre-registered rule;
this is not evidence of absence. A blind confirmation of QD was not run,
by decision. On OLD31, IT-D $-$ DK has Holm $p=0.123$, and about 80\% of
it comes from the 18 questions that needed one appended passage: a single
context-conditioned rewrite, neither iteration nor a graph.

\label{sec:rebuiltmech}

detectable gain. They are descriptive readings of the same development
data, not tested causal hypotheses.

\textbf{(A) Selection.} The cross-encoder scores a passage only against
the question or sub-query. Where it fills ten slots against the full
question, as in GD, it keeps missing hops from the graph arm's pool at
about the rate it keeps them from the control's (31 of 59 vs.\ 17 of 35;
Table~\ref{tab:rebuiltmech}). Where it fills only $m$ appended slots or
scores against a per-hop sub-query, graph-proposed bridge passages reach
the pool but rarely survive: the L0 pool held 37 of 58 missing hops and 8
were kept, and in the per-hop probe, gold passages brought in by the
graph were kept 0 times. In that probe, bridge passages scored mostly
$10^{-3}$--$10^{-2}$,
near the bottom of their pools (\eg \code{mal.1.1} ranked 20 of 20 at
0.0009, \code{1ki.16.15} 15 of 21 at 0.011). Of 21 PPR-brought bridge
gold passages (14 hops), only the gold of a single hop outscored the best
dense candidate of its step; their median score was 0.013 against 0.417. Feeding graph
signals into selection made it worse: reciprocal-rank fusion of the
reranker with PPR or with name matches kept 19 and 14 hops, against 21
for the reranker alone. On October 18, IT-GD's PPR candidates held a
new-hop gold passage absent from IT-D's pool in 23 of 164 steps, but the
reranker chose a PPR candidate 10 times, 2 of them hitting a new hop. This
mirrors Round~3, where wrong graph anchors with surface overlap passed
the same reranker (Section~\ref{sec:round3mech}). HippoRAG~2 instead
ranks passages by the PPR scores themselves~\cite{gutierrez2025hipporag2};
we did not reproduce that design.

\textbf{(B) No identity or relation layer, and low coverage.} Rebuild-2
has neither layer, and its name keys merge homonyms. On the rebuilt text
the kinship prototype (Genesis, Ruth, 1--2~Samuel, 1--2~Kings,
1~Chronicles~1--9, Matthew~1, Luke~3) gives two or more distinct
fathers (or parents of unstated sex) to 163 of 622 children (26.2\%),
and 703 of its 801 cross-passage two-hop chains pass through such a node; the KIN pool
reaches 17 of 58 missing hops. The legacy KG was no better: its
\code{entity\_id} (type plus toneless pinyin) produced 266 colliding ids,
and its 15{,}926 semantic edges had a strict precision of about
0.23--0.28 (Appendix~\ref{app:rebuild}). On the 84 GT~v2 questions with a
strong need for the KG it answered 25 completely, 44 partly, and 15
wrongly; it was wrong on 11 of 13 identity-disambiguation questions and
on the only multi-hop question. On GN-41 it gave correct names for 9 of
104 answer-entity groups (3 strictly), 81 misses being missing data and
14 mechanism, and it produced any name for only 13 of 41 questions. This
is the incomplete-knowledge regime in which KG-based RAG is known to
degrade~\cite{zhou2025brink}.

\textbf{(C) Little graph-unique headroom, overlap with non-graph
channels, and winner's curse.} GD at $k=10$ reached 14 missing hops that C
missed (none the other way), but only about 6 of the 102 are reachable by
the graph and by no non-graph channel (the script behind this count was
not archived), and GD's lead over character-bigram BM25 and lexical
pseudo-relevance feedback ($+0.052$, $+0.050$ at $k=10$) would have failed
its Holm step ($p=0.0254>0.025$) even had the primary test been confirmed.
Every apparent win over plain dense retrieval of a graph arm that
includes an LLM rewrite came from the rewrite, which the non-graph twin
reproduces: QD+G, QD+N, and QD beat DEEP by $+0.082$, $+0.099$, and
$+0.101$; QD+N and QD, which differ only in the name line, differ by
$-0.002$ $[-0.037, +0.038]$, an interval as wide as those of G1 and G3;
and IT-D beat C7 by $+0.100$ $[+0.047, +0.155]$, to which PPR candidates
added $+0.010$. GD's single-shot win at $k=10$ involves no LLM; we
attribute it to the small graph-unique headroom and to selection on the
development set. GD was picked from about ten settings on NEW82, where
222 of 223 hops have a gold passage containing a body name (OLD31: 100
of 101); its gain depends on PPR's preference for name-dense passages
(degree-normalized: $+0.006$ $[-0.030, +0.045]$ at $k=10$); and 11:0 on
NEW82 became 2:2 on OLD31, the winner's curse that the pre-registration
had flagged in its power analysis. Other explanations --- OLD31's
different population, only four discordant clusters, a smaller true
effect --- are not excluded. The picture is consistent with graph benefit
being task-dependent~\cite{xiang2025whengraphs} and with GraphRAG gains
shrinking under unbiased evaluation~\cite{zeng2025unbiased}.

\subsection{Closure and What It Does Not Claim}
\label{sec:rebuiltclosure}

\textbf{What was closed.} Graph-retrieval variants on the current graph
layer are closed. They were measured on the L0 name--passage graph: GD
was stopped by V1, QD+G by rule G, and IT-GD, which rule I alone did not
stop, was closed with them because the pre-run ruling tied the closure
to rule G. The L1 event registry and the kinship surface triples,
measured earlier (the event lookup lane on GT~v2; KIN on OLD31), are
closed by the authors' pre-run ruling. After the GD stop, no further single-shot
graph variant was opened.

\textbf{What this does not claim.} The stops are not evidence of absence.
Power is low: V1 rested on 4 discordant clusters of 20 and stops under
the null with probability 0.552; the planned follow-up procedure, which
would have rejected the null with probability 0.825 at a cluster win
rate of 0.8, never ran; and realkg and iterq could detect only effects
of 0.085--0.179. The data are development sets: NEW82 was seen and GD
tuned on it, OLD31 is only partially blind, and routing and the event
triggers were tuned on GT~v2. The multi-hop sets are LLM-authored and
unreviewed, and their answers were graded by LLM raters. The realkg and
iterq intervals are over questions although OLD31 has a cluster of 9
questions, so they may be too narrow. GD and the October 18 arms were
measured offline at the retrieval stage only. On October 18 only the L0
graph was exercised, and no layer with entity identity or reliable
relations has been tested, so the result concerns this graph layer, not
graph retrieval in general. The closure also covers designs we did not
reproduce on this layer, notably ranking passages by PPR scores without
the cross-encoder, as HippoRAG~2 does (mechanism~(A)); it is a decision
about further work, not a result about them.

\textbf{Reopening condition.} Reopening requires a new graph layer
(\eg entity identity, reliable relations) and a new pre-registration;
under the attribution rule, a reopened test must again beat both a
$k$-aligned dense arm and a non-graph twin.
